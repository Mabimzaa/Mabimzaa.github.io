Anatomy of an attack

Most attacks don't start with hacking. They start with an internet search. Here is a common scenario, step by step.

  1. Reconnaissance

    The attacker lists your staff on LinkedIn and rebuilds your org chart in under an hour.

    What an audit would have done

    Spot what is exposed and help teams control what they publish.

  2. Guessing email addresses

    A single public address is enough to guess the firstname.lastname@ format for the whole team.

    What an audit would have done

    Identify guessable addresses and check anti-spoofing protections (SPF, DMARC).

  3. Breach lookup

    They find an employee's old password in a public data breach.

    What an audit would have done

    Detect the affected accounts and enforce unique passwords with two-factor authentication.

  4. Spear phishing

    They send a convincing email that mentions a real project and a real manager's name.

    What an audit would have done

    Train teams to recognise this kind of message, with realistic examples.

  5. Compromise

    One click, one password typed in: the mailbox or network is open.

    What an audit would have done

    Break the chain at step one, long before it gets here.

Frequently asked questions

What people ask me most before we start.

Is OSINT legal?

Yes, as long as it sticks to publicly available information and respects data protection law (GDPR). I never access a system without authorisation and never bypass any protection.

What are you likely to find about me?

It depends on your exposure. Most often: email addresses, accounts in old breaches, information about your teams and part of your technical infrastructure.

How long does an audit take?

It depends on the scope. We define it together at the start, and you know the timeline before work begins.

What happens to the data collected?

It is used only for the report, kept confidential and deleted at the end of the engagement.

Do you work in French too?

Yes. Conversations and reports can be in English or French.
